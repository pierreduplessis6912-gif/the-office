# Credit notes: issue

Capability-map row 3 (`docs/CAPABILITY_MAP.md`), the next one in Pierre's order after quotations.

## How the three questions were decided, and what the research concluded
Pierre was asked three accounting questions (does a credit note reduce what a customer owes by being allocated to an invoice; what happens to the tax; can it exceed the invoice). He first answered **"Use best practice in these scenarios"** (2026-11-10), then asked for the best practice to be **researched and the open questions concluded**. The research below was done on 2026-11-10 from SARS's own material, a major accounting firm's VAT note, a court report, and the documented behaviour of widely used accounting systems. **It is research and a reasoned conclusion, not tax or legal advice; Claude is not an accountant. The residual items in "Still genuinely open" need SARS wording or an accountant, and are named so they are not mistaken for settled.**

### What the sources say
- **What a South African tax credit note must show (VAT Act s21(3)(a), as set out by BDO, 2022):** the words "credit note" prominently; the vendor's name, address and VAT number; the recipient's name and address (and VAT number if a vendor); the date issued; the amount the value was reduced by and the excess tax (or, where tax is included in the price, the reduction and the tax rate); a brief explanation of the circumstances; and information sufficient to identify the transaction it refers to (the usual reading is the original tax invoice's serial number). BDO notes the last two are the ones most often missed. Source: https://bdo.co.za/en-za/insights/2022/tax/credit-notes-the-unseen-danger-953e7fe53ad5a9a3426513277d83f32f
- **When a credit note is the right instrument:** the vendor must issue one when the consideration for a supply is reduced relative to the original tax invoice (SARS Interpretation Note 56, 2010: https://www.sars.gov.za/lapd-intr-in-2012-56-recipient-created-tax-invoices-credit-debit-notes ). A credit note may only be issued if output tax was incorrectly accounted for on an invoice; the supplier reduces output tax in the tax period in which that becomes apparent, and the recipient adjusts in the period the credit note is issued (Business Day, 2024: https://www.pressreader.com/south-africa/business-day/20240513/282106346736189 ).
- **Timing:** section 21 prescribes no period between the original charge and the later adjustment (Tax Court report: https://www.moonstone.co.za/?p=59982 ). So there is no statutory deadline to enforce, but the credit note's date decides which VAT period it falls in.
- **Credit note versus refund:** across accounting systems and guides, a credit note is the document that reduces what the customer owes (or creates a credit balance); a refund is a separate act of returning money. When the invoice is already paid, the credit becomes a balance in the customer's favour that is later applied to a future invoice or refunded; when the invoice is unpaid or part-paid, it reduces the balance due, with any excess handled the same way (QuickBooks: https://quickbooks.intuit.com/learn-support/en-sg/manage-customers-and-income/enter-and-apply-a-credit-note-or-delayed-credit/01/382841 ; Manager.io: https://www2.manager.io/guides/7425 ; Moss glossary: https://www.getmoss.com/en-gb/glossary/credit-note ).
- **Retention:** a credit note must be kept for the period in the Tax Administration Act and VAT Act s55 (five years); the engine never deletes a fact, so this holds by construction.

### Conclusions on each decision
1. **Reduce what is owed by applying the credit to the invoice (decision 1): CONFIRMED.** It is the standard treatment; the invoice stays as issued and the credit note adjusts it. Keeping the credit note, the movement and the allocation as separate facts matches "a credit note reduces what is owed; a refund moves money".
2. **Tax inherited from the invoice line (decision 2): CONFIRMED.** The s21 adjustment is the excess tax on the reduced value at the rate originally charged, so inheriting the rate is both correct and safer than choosing it again. The engine already shows net, tax and total on the credit note, which is the s21(3)(a)(v) content.
3. **Never more than was invoiced, cumulatively, and never at a higher price (decision 3): CONFIRMED** as a control. A credit note is only valid where the invoice reflected too much output tax, so crediting beyond the invoice has no VAT basis; a price increase belongs on a debit note, which is not built.
4. **Reason required, not dated before the invoice (decision 4): CONFIRMED.** The "brief explanation" is a statutory particular (s21(3)(a)(vi)); the invoice link and number satisfy "information sufficient to identify the transaction" (vii). The engine requires and records both.
5. **Only an invoice can be credited (decision 5): CONFIRMED.**
6. **Excess stays as an unapplied credit, never silently refunded (decision 6): CONFIRMED, and one gap fixed.** The sources agree the credit is a balance owed to the customer, applied later or refunded. The research showed the gap: the credit was recorded but not visible on the customer's account, so a customer owed R575.00 looked like "owes nothing". **Fixed in this change:** the customer's account (`receivables.query`) and record now show `unappliedCreditMinor` ("Credit owed back") beside what they owe, never netted into it. This also surfaces any overpayment, as the slice's overpayment scenario now shows (R150.00).
7. **Credit notes are issued by money roles only (decision 7): kept** (an internal control, not a legal requirement).
8. **An issued credit makes earlier proposals about the customer stale (decision 8): kept** (an engine safety property).
9. **No schema migration (decision 9): kept.**
10. **The invoice's `Paid` includes credits, `Credited` shown beside it (decision 10): CONFIRMED** as a presentation choice that keeps settled value and credited value distinguishable.

### What the engine holds against the s21(3)(a) list, and what is still missing
Held: the "credit note" kind, the date, the reduced value and tax (net, tax, total), the reason, the link to and number of the original invoice, the recipient's name and (since customers and suppliers) address. **Not held by the engine at all:** the vendor's own name, address and VAT number (a business setting), and the recipient's VAT number. These belong to the document that is SENT (the PDF or email), which is not built; **a credit note must not be sent to a customer as a tax document until it carries them.** A future test should assert the full s21(3)(a) list on the rendered document.

### Still genuinely open (needs SARS wording or an accountant, not more research)
- **Whether and how VAT periods are reported.** The engine has no VAT return or period lock, so a credit note dated into an already-filed period is not flagged. Accountant's decision on period handling when VAT reporting is built.
- **Abridged invoices (supplies under R5,000):** the recipient's particulars may be omitted on a credit note for such a supply (SARS BGR 72 describes this for one case); the engine does not distinguish and always records what it has. Low risk; confirm when documents are built.
- **Rounding on credited lines:** the engine rounds each line as it does for invoices; whether a credit of the full invoice should reproduce the invoice's tax to the cent in every rounding case is untested beyond the cases above.
- **Debit notes** (increasing an invoice), **applying an unapplied credit to another invoice**, **a refund**, and **voiding a credit note** are real, separate capabilities the sources describe; none is built, and none is assumed.

## What it is
- **`credit_note.issue`** (held write): credits ONE invoice, line by line, for a stated reason. Each credited line names an invoice line (`invoiceLineNo`), a quantity (in thousandths) and a unit price (in cents). It records, in one transaction: the credit note document (kind `credit_note`, its own number sequence), a non-cash movement (direction `in`, method `credit`) for its total, an allocation of the part still owed on the invoice, and a `credit_note.issued` event tying them to the invoice. The approval is shown the exact net, tax and total the engine worked out.
- It is reached through the proposals door and opened through records: the invoice shows `Credited` and a link to each credit note; the credit note shows its reason, what was applied, what was left unapplied, and which invoice it credits. There is no separate find route yet.

## Decisions taken (best practice, then researched; each open to change by Pierre)
1. **A credit note reduces what is owed by being applied to the invoice** as a non-cash movement allocated to it (the pattern the fit test walked), so the invoice's `outstanding` falls and its status moves toward paid. The invoice document itself is never edited; what is owed, credited and left over is derived.
2. **Tax is inherited, never chosen.** Every credited line takes the tax rate of the invoice line it credits, so the VAT credited is exactly proportional. A credit line that supplies its own tax rate is refused.
3. **A line can never be credited for more than it was worth,** counting all earlier credit notes (cumulative net per invoice line, at most the line's original net), and not at a higher unit price than it was charged. So a credit note can never exceed the invoice. Crediting exactly what remains is allowed.
4. **A reason is required** (up to 200 characters), and a credit note cannot be dated before its invoice.
5. **Only an invoice can be credited** (a document of a kind the customer owes for, direction `sell`, not reversed). A quotation, or a credit note, cannot.
6. **The excess stays as an unapplied credit.** If the invoice was already paid (fully or partly), only what is still owed is applied; the rest of the credit is recorded as unapplied on the customer (`unappliedMinor` on the credit note), shown on the customer's account as `unappliedCreditMinor` beside (never netted into) what they owe, and is **never silently refunded or spent**. Refunding or applying it to another invoice is a separate capability, not built.
7. **Permission** (engine vocabulary): `credit_note.issue`, granted to `can_manage_invoices` only (owner, accountant, admin). A salesperson and an installer cannot, even though a salesperson can make quotes: a credit note is a money correction. Proposing and approving both need it.
8. **An earlier proposal about the customer or the invoice becomes stale** when a credit note is issued (a payment proposed before a credit must be proposed again), and two people cannot both credit the same amount past the invoice.
9. **No schema migration.** Everything uses existing fact tables and a new event type, `credit_note.issued`, which the integrity verifier knows and checks (the credit note, invoice and movement must exist; applied plus unapplied must equal the credit note's total and its movement).
10. **The invoice record's `Paid` figure includes credits.** `Credited` is shown beside it so the two can be told apart.

## Not in this capability (each a separate decision, none assumed)
Voiding or reversing a credit note, a refund to the customer, applying an unapplied credit to another invoice, supplier credit notes (purchases), rounding rules beyond the engine's existing per-line arithmetic, the PDF, and how a credit note affects VAT returns or period reporting (no VAT reporting exists on the engine).
