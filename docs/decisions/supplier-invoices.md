# Supplier invoices, the three-way match and supplier payment

## What other systems do (see purchase-orders.md for the sources)
A supplier invoice (bill) is matched to the purchase order and to what was actually received (the three-way match); an invoice that agrees within tolerance posts and can be paid, an exception (a different price, more billed than received, a line that was never ordered) is held for a person to resolve; a bill with no order is routed for coding and approval; payment is blocked above the received quantity; a paid expense differs from a bill because a bill is a payable (Xero bills, QuickBooks bills, Odoo vendor bills). The engine already holds the money side: a bill is a `supplier_invoice` document (a payable kind), and a payment is a money-out movement allocated to it (checked: paying R500.00 of a R1,150.00 bill leaves R650.00 outstanding).

## Decisions (defaults)
1. **A bill is a document** of kind `supplier_invoice` (direction buy, a payable), recorded by a held write with the supplier's invoice number (required; the same number from the same supplier twice is refused as a duplicate), invoice date, optional due date, a category from the expense list, whether a valid tax invoice is held, an optional `capital` flag, and lines (description, quantity, unit price, explicit tax rate; VAT is never assumed). An optional order reference makes it a three-way match; each line may name the order line it bills (`orderLineNo`).
2. **The match is derived and shown on approval, never decided silently.** With an order: a line is a VARIANCE if its price differs from the order's price, its tax rate differs, it bills more than has been RECEIVED (counting what earlier bills already billed on that order line), or it is not on the order. Strict: no tolerance in this version (a tolerance is a later business setting; typical practice is 1 to 5%). No variance: `matched`. Any variance: `variance`. No order: `no_order` (allowed: services and ad-hoc purchases). The match result and every variance are stamped on the proposal so the approver sees them.
3. **A bill with a variance is recorded but cannot be paid until its variance is accepted.** The proposer sets `varianceAccepted: true` to say "I have checked this and accept it"; a person still approves the proposal, so the acceptance is a recorded, explained decision. A bill that is `matched` or `no_order` is payable at once. This follows the practice of blocking payment of exceptions while still letting the invoice be entered.
4. **A bill's order must be with the same supplier, and an order that was cancelled can still be billed against** (the bill is then a quantity variance: nothing was received). Receipts and bills never edit the order.
5. **Supplier payment** (`supplier_payment.record`): a money-out movement allocated to the supplier's payable bills, fully (no unapplied supplier credit in this version, and no overpayment to a supplier). The person names the bills and amounts, or says `oldest_first`; each allocation is at most what the bill still owes; a bill that is not payable (an unaccepted variance, or voided) is refused; the payment date cannot be before a bill's date; method is one of eft, cash, card, cheque, other.
6. **Void** (`supplier_invoice.void`, reason required): reverses the bill; refused while any payment is allocated to it (remove the allocation first), and refused when its claimed input tax is in a locked tax period (it would change a filed return).
7. **Input tax** follows the expense rules exactly (`docs/decisions/expenses.md`): claimable only if the business is VAT registered, the supplier is a vendor with a VAT number on record, a valid tax invoice is held (above R50), the category is not denied by s17(2), and there is tax. The claim date is fixed WHEN THE BILL IS RECORDED (the invoice date, or the first open day after the tax lock). **The VAT summary now includes bills** in fields 14 and 15 and lists bills whose tax was not claimed, with reasons. A bill is a tax document of the SUPPLIER, not of this business, so the completeness check does not apply to it.
8. **Permissions:** `bill.manage` (record and void) and `supplier_payment.record` from `can_manage_invoices` only (money roles); `bill.read` from `can_manage_invoices` and the legacy `can_know_supplier_balances`. Field staff do not enter or pay bills.
9. **Derived, not stored:** a bill's status (`unpaid`, `part_paid`, `paid`, `voided`), whether it is payable and what it still owes come from the ledger and the `supplier_invoice.recorded` event; no schema migration.

## Not in this capability (each a separate decision, none assumed)
A tolerance for the match, supplier credit notes and returns, an unapplied supplier credit or a prepayment, early-payment discounts, recurring bills, an approval limit on payments, bank-feed matching and the remittance advice, foreign-currency bills, and the supplier statement reconciliation.

## Findings from building it
- **The money layer needed no change:** a bill is an ordinary payable document and a supplier payment an ordinary money-out movement allocated to it (probed first: R500.00 against a R1,150.00 bill left R650.00 owing); the new capabilities add the rules around them.
- **The match is worked out when the bill is APPROVED, not when it is proposed:** a second bill proposed as matched becomes a quantity variance if the first is approved in between (everything received was already billed), and then cannot be paid; the proposal's stamped match is only what the approver is shown.
- **`oldest_first` skips a bill with an unaccepted variance** instead of failing the whole payment, and says plainly when what is payable falls short of the amount.
- **The VAT summary's input side now covers expenses and bills**, sharing one set of claim rules (`expenses.inputTaxWith`); supplier credit notes, imports and adjustments are still not in it.
- **Open for Pierre:** a match tolerance (strict for now), supplier credit notes and returns, and an approval limit on supplier payments.
