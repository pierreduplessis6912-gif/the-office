# Purchase orders and goods received

## What other systems do (secondary sources; the practice is consistent across them)
- The **purchase order** is the source of truth for what was agreed (lines, quantities, prices, terms); the **goods receipt** records what physically arrived; the supplier **invoice** is then matched to both (the three-way match). Partial deliveries are normal: receive only what arrived and never inflate to the expected quantity, or the match breaks later (https://www.cleverence.com/articles/for-business/complete-purchase-order-6734/ ; https://www.cleverence.com/articles/for-business/what-is-the-process-of-purchase-order-4827/ ).
- **Over-receipt needs a control**: either a tolerance (for example 2%) or strict exact matching (https://www.cleverence.com/articles/business-blogs/odoo-receiving-4827/ ). A short delivery leaves a backorder for the balance; the order closes when everything is received and invoiced or the rest is cancelled.
- Matching rules typically **block payment above the received quantity**, with a tolerance of 1 to 5% routing variances for review (https://ecosire.com/blog/accounting-automation-bookkeeping-guide-2026 ; https://www.cleverence.com/articles/for-business/purchase-order-accounting-entry-4827/ ). That belongs to the second half (supplier invoices).

## Decisions (defaults)
1. **A purchase order is a document** of kind `purchase_order` (direction buy, from a supplier, lines with an explicit tax rate, optional expected date and supplier reference). It is a commitment, **not a payable and not a tax document**: it never touches money, the tax lock or the VAT summary. Creating it is a held write a person approves.
2. **A goods receipt is a document** of kind `goods_receipt` (its own number sequence) that records what arrived against ONE order's lines, with a `goods.received` event. Money is never involved. Receiving is a held write.
3. **Status is derived, never stored, and nothing is edited:** `open` (nothing received), `part_received`, `received` (every line complete), `cancelled` (the remainder cancelled with nothing received) or `closed` (the remainder cancelled after part was received: closed short). No schema migration (the doctrine of parties.ts, quotations.ts and expenses.ts).
4. **Receive only what arrived, never more than ordered (strict).** A line cannot be received beyond its remaining quantity; the refusal says what remains. There is no tolerance in this first version (a tolerance is a business setting, a later decision); a supplier who delivers more is handled by a new order for the excess (revising an order is not built).
5. **Cancel closes the remainder.** `purchase_order.cancel` (a reason is required) cancels what has not been received; if nothing was received the order is `cancelled`, if part was received it is `closed` short. An order that is fully received, or already cancelled or closed, cannot be cancelled again. Goods can no longer be received on a cancelled or closed order.
6. **Dates:** a receipt cannot be dated before its order; the engine has no clock, so a date is always stated.
7. **Permissions** (engine vocabulary): `purchase.manage` (create and cancel orders) from `can_manage_invoices` only; `purchase.read` and `goods.receive` from `can_manage_invoices` and `can_know_materials` (field staff). **Field staff may PROPOSE a receipt but not approve it**: confirming a goods receipt needs `can_manage_invoices` (a money role), so the person on site says what arrived and a manager approves it. (The legacy rule let either capability confirm; this is stricter, deliberately.)
8. **Stale protection:** a proposal carries the version of the order it was made against; an approval after the order changed (another receipt, a cancellation) is refused, so two people cannot both receive the last of a line.
9. **Reading:** `purchase_orders.find` lists orders with their status, ordered, received and outstanding values and per-line progress; the order's record shows the lines with received and remaining quantities and links to its receipts; a receipt's record links back to its order. All derived.

## Not in this capability (each a separate decision, none assumed)
Supplier invoices and the three-way match, supplier payment, price and quantity variances and tolerances, revising an order, receipt of services versus goods, returns to the supplier and supplier credit notes, stock on hand and valuation (Inventory), approval limits and requisitions, and printing or sending the order (Documents).

## Findings from building it
- **Field staff are legitimately in the engine now.** Mapping the legacy materials capability (`can_know_materials`) to `purchase.read` and `goods.receive` means an installer can read orders and PROPOSE a receipt (and over MCP is offered the purchasing tools only); approving still needs a money role. The MCP test that said an installer is offered nothing was wrong from this point and was corrected.
- **A stale approval is a different thing from an over-receipt.** Two people receiving 3 of 10 sheets each would both fit the order, but the second approval is still refused because the order changed after it was proposed; that rule was only visible once a test used quantities that fit.
- **Open for Pierre:** whether over-receipt should have a tolerance (a business setting; strict for now), whether orders can be revised (not built), and whether a purchase order should need an approval threshold (not built).
