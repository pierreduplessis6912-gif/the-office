Speak into the Ether

A cognitive prosthetic for people who hold too much

The plumber already talks to himself. Between the truck and the job site, while holding the drill, after the call with the angry customer. The Ether listens. It remembers. It reacts. It tells you what matters so you can breathe.

01 — Philosophy

The plumber doesn't go to the office. The office comes to him.

Traditional business software is built for desks. Forms, dashboards, dropdowns, date pickers. A plumber has dirty hands, noisy environments, and no patience for context switching. Zero adoption is the default.

The Ether inverts this. The plumber already mutters, narrates, complains, celebrates. He speaks into the void constantly. The only change: now the void remembers.

"Peter. Eshowe is handled. The venue part is ordered. Your wife confirmed the Danish travel. You have 45 minutes before crew drop-off. Dog food is on your route. Do not take another job this week. Breathe."

— The Ether, 6:47 PM

🎙️ Voice is the hero

One big red button. Hold to talk. Release to send. No keyboard by default. Text is a debug mode you intentionally summon. The interface is ears first, eyes second.

🛡️ Guard everything

Every mutation — payment, new customer, address update — lands in a holding table first. A human must confirm before anything touches ground truth. The LLM proposes. Peter decides.

🧠 Memory is a hierarchy

KV for instant recall. D1 for structured facts. Progressive summaries for narrative compression. Entity graph for relationships. Vectorize for cross-customer search. Each layer has a purpose.

💨 The sigh is the metric

Success is not API latency or test coverage. Success is the moment Peter opens the app and feels relief instead of overwhelm. The green dot. "Nothing urgent. Breathe."

🔒 Git is the source of truth

The Cloudflare dashboard is read-only. If it's not in a commit, it isn't real. Secrets never live in the repo. Names go in config files; values live in encrypted secret stores.

🏗️ Build order is sacred

Shell → mic → D1 → guard → memory → briefing. Each step is usable tomorrow. Do not build the cathedral before the foundation. Do not build 5 before 3 is solid.

04 — Principles

Every decision flows from one question: does this help Peter breathe?

  1. 01

    One screen, no tabs

    Everything is gesture or voice. Swipe right for history. Swipe left for menu. The main screen is sacred — only the briefing, the response, and the mic.

  2. 02

    The mic is the biggest thing on screen

    72px diameter, center-bottom, 80% of thumb reach. It dominates the visual hierarchy. Color states: dark red (idle), bright pulsing red (recording), amber (processing), green flash (success).

  3. 03

    Text is always secondary

    Smaller, gray, below the audio. The waveform visualizes speech. Text is a fallback for noisy environments. Tap text to copy — a hidden affordance, not a primary action.

  4. 04

    The briefing is ambient

    There when needed, invisible when not. Collapsed to counts by default. Red dot if critical. Auto-collapses after 10 seconds of inactivity. Never demands attention.

  5. 05

    Responses are spoken first

    TTS auto-plays. Waveform animates. Text appears after. In a truck, on a roof, with noisy tools — Peter hears before he reads.

  6. 06

    No notifications

    The app doesn't buzz. Peter opens it when he wants to. The Ether is a tool, not a boss. It waits for him. It never demands.

  7. 07

    No onboarding

    Open app → hold button → talk. That's it. No account creation flow. No tutorial. No "let's get started" screens. The first voice note is the onboarding.

  8. 08

    No settings on first launch

    Defaults are tuned for a tradesperson in a truck. Dark mode. Large touch targets. Voice on. English. If Peter needs to change something, he can find it later. But he shouldn't need to on day one.

  9. 09

    Dark mode default

    Easier on eyes in bright sunlight. Less battery drain on OLED. The void is dark. The Ether lives in the void.

  10. 10

    Large touch targets

    Gloves. Dirty hands. One-handed use while holding a drill. Every tap target is at least 48px. The mic is 72px. Buttons are generous. Precision is not expected.

05 — Anti-Patterns

This is NOT...

Every product category The Ether deliberately avoids. If it looks like any of these, it has failed.

✕

NOT a chat app with a mic button

WhatsApp, Telegram — chat bubbles, typing indicators, read receipts, conversational back-and-forth. The Ether is not a conversation. It is a monologue that gets understood.

✕

NOT a form with voice fill

Siri Shortcuts — "Hey Siri, add a payment for Jenny for 850 rand." The Ether has no forms. There are no fields. There is no structure imposed on the user. Just speak.

✕

NOT a dashboard with a voice widget

Google Assistant, Alexa — a voice layer bolted onto a screen-first interface. The Ether has no dashboard. No charts. No KPIs. No customer list as the default view.

✕

NOT a note-taking app with voice input

Notion, Obsidian — capture everything, organize later. The Ether doesn't capture. It understands. It compresses. It tells you what matters. It is not a filing cabinet.

✕

NOT an AI that talks back

ChatGPT, Claude — conversational, exploratory, open-ended. The Ether is not a chatbot. It does not ask "How can I help you today?" It listens, holds, and speaks only when it has something worth saying.

06 — The Journey

From muttering to breathing

The path a user takes from first open to daily reliance. Each stage is a commitment, not a feature.

First Open

App opens to a dark screen with a single red circle. No tutorial. No "get started." Peter holds the button, says "test," releases. The system responds: "Got it." That's the onboarding.

0 seconds to first value

First Real Note

Peter is in the truck, between jobs. He holds the button: "Jenny paid 850." The system extracts the payment, holds it for confirmation, speaks back: "Payment noted for Jenny of R850 — needs your confirmation." Peter taps confirm. The first ground-truth write happens.

Guard discipline proven

First Briefing

Peter opens the app at 6 PM. The briefing card shows: "3 things need your attention." He taps. Payment #7 pending. FedEx clearance reminder. Eshowe daughter pressure. He taps the payment, confirms it. He feels the first hint of relief.

The snapshot becomes real

First Sigh

A calm day. Peter opens the app. The briefing card is gone. The screen is green. "Nothing urgent. Breathe." He exhales. He didn't realize he was holding tension until the app told him he could let go.

The product proves its worth

Daily Ritual

Peter no longer thinks about The Ether. He just speaks into it. In the truck, on the roof, after the angry call. The muttering is the same. But now the muttering has a memory. And sometimes, at the end of a hard day, the app tells him to breathe.

Cognitive load externalized

Dependence

Peter forgets a commitment. He asks The Ether: "What did I promise the Eshowe lady?" The system traverses the entity graph, finds the promise, speaks it back. Peter realizes he no longer holds his business in his head. The Ether holds it for him.
